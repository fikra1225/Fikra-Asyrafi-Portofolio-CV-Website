Project 01 — Compensation Analytics

Indeed Salaries Workbook

Excel Workbook · 9 Sheets · Multi-Company Salary Data

● Completed

📋 Project Overview

This workbook analyzes 979 rows of median salary data scraped from Indeed, spanning 98 US companies and 29 job categories. The goal was to demonstrate a realistic, end-to-end Excel analysis workflow: clean the raw data, normalize pay periods with lookup functions, summarize with pivot tables, run sensitivity and goal-seek analysis on pay raises, and automate reporting with VBA.

Salaries were originally reported on inconsistent bases — hourly, weekly, monthly, or yearly — so a core piece of the project was building a reliable Annual Salary figure that makes every role comparable.

  • Data Source: Raw CSV of Indeed salary listings, untouched in an Excel Table
  • Normalization: INDEX/MATCH against a period-to-multiplier lookup table
  • Summarization: Formula-built and native PivotTables by category and company
  • Analysis: Raise sensitivity table, Goal Seek, and Scenario Manager
  • Automation: Three VBA macros for cleaning, reporting, and dashboard refresh

Project 02 — Consumer Price Analytics

US Prices Workbook

Excel Workbook · 6 Sheets · BLS Average Price Data

● Completed

📋 Project Overview

This workbook tracks 7,814 monthly price observations for 74 grocery and energy items from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics Average Price Data series, spanning January 2015 through July 2026. The goal was to go beyond a single lookup table and build a small analytics pipeline: a long-format source table, a per-item summary with coverage and trend metrics, a wide-pivoted view for direct item-to-item comparison, an interactive dashboard, and a from-scratch regression model.

Because BLS discontinues and introduces series over time, a key part of the project was tracking is_current status so trend comparisons only run on items with a full, comparable history.

  • Data Source: US BLS Average Price Data (public domain, CC0), untouched in an Excel Table
  • Summarization: Per-item coverage, min/max, and 12-month average % change
  • Lookups: Single-key INDEX/MATCH and a 3-column composite array-match lookup
  • Modeling: SLOPE/INTERCEPT/RSQ regression, sensitivity table, and Goal Seek
  • Automation: Scaffolded VBA macros for category rollups, conditional formatting, and refresh

Data Insights

Key Findings from the Price Data

☕

Biggest Mover: Coffee

Ground roast coffee rose 96.29% on a trailing 12-month average basis — the largest increase of any tracked item, ahead of frozen orange juice concentrate (+77.93%) and piped utility gas (+77.35%).

📇

Most Stable: Pasta

Spaghetti and macaroni moved just +0.15% over the same window — effectively flat across more than a decade of data, in sharp contrast to volatile categories like Beef and Energy.

⚠

Discontinued Series

14 of the 74 tracked items (19%) — including Broccoli and Frankfurters — were discontinued by BLS mid-series, which is exactly why the is_current flag matters before trusting a trend.

⛽

Gas & Milk Move Together

A simple linear regression finds gasoline prices explain ~59% of the variation in milk price (R² = 0.589): every $1/gal rise in gas predicts roughly a $0.50/gal rise in milk.

Under the Hood

Excel Techniques & Functions Applied

The formulas, models, and automation demonstrated across the workbook.

INDEX/MATCH (single key)

Looks up category, unit, first/last price, min/max, and % change from ItemSummary by item name.

INDEX/MATCH (composite key)

An array formula matching item, year, and month simultaneously — (A=x)*(B=y)*(C=z) — to pull one exact monthly price.

Excel Tables

tblMonthly, tblItemSummary, and tblWide keep every formula and lookup range self-expanding and readable.

SLOPE / INTERCEPT / RSQ

Builds a linear regression of gasoline price against milk price directly from the WideSelected data, no add-ins required.

Sensitivity Table

Projects predicted milk price across eight gasoline-price scenarios from $2.00 to $5.50 per gallon.

Goal Seek

Set up to solve for the exact gasoline price that would predict a target $4.50 milk price.

Live What-If Lever

Editing a single hypothetical gasoline price cell instantly recalculates the predicted milk price.

Coverage & Trend Metrics

First/last date, min/max price, and 12-month average % change computed per item to flag discontinued series.

VBA: ExportCategorySummary

Scaffolded macro to prompt for a category, roll up item counts and average % change, and export it to a new sheet.

VBA: HighlightPriceChanges

Scaffolded macro to color-code every item row by its 12-month % change — red, amber, or green.

VBA: RefreshAll

Scaffolded macro to refresh every PivotTable in the workbook and force a full recalculation in one click.

Note: the three VBA macros above are included as structured practice exercises — each sub is scaffolded with a clear goal and step-by-step TODOs (InputBox prompts, ListObject column lookups, conditional formatting rules, and PivotTable refresh logic) rather than shipped as finished code, as a way of documenting the intended automation design.
