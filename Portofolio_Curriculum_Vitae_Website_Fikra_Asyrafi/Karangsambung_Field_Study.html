Geology of Karangsambung, Kebumen, Central Java

Group field mapping of an accretionary-complex terrane where igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic rocks all outcrop within a single, walkable area.

Geomorphology Stratigraphy Structural Geology Field Survey Technical Reporting
Location Karangsambung, Kebumen, Central Java, Indonesia
Course Field Geology (GL 3204)
Institution Bandung Institute of Technology (ITB)
Timeline Jun – Aug 2023

Chapter I: Project Overview

Why Karangsambung

An ideal natural laboratory where the full rock cycle is exposed in one field area.

Karangsambung is one of Indonesia's richest areas for geological diversity — a rare location where igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic rocks all occur together, alongside structures that are easy to observe in the field. As part of the mandatory Geological Field Course (GL 3204) at ITB, I collaborated with peers to collect, validate, and synthesize primary field data to reconstruct the geological history of the area.

The methodology followed a standard geological mapping workflow: first interpreting geomorphology (landform units) from topographic patterns, then validating rock and structural boundaries through direct field observation, and finally analyzing the collected lithological and structural data to compile a complete geological map.

The result is a dataset of eight geomorphological units, six rock units, and two structural types — enough to tell a coherent story of how this corner of Java was assembled, from deep subduction-zone mixing to present-day river deposition.

Chapter II

Geomorphology

Landform analysis is the first step of any geological map — contrasts in slope, contour spacing, and drainage pattern hint at what lies beneath before a single outcrop is checked.

⛰

Homoclinal Hills

Steep hills in the center and south, gentler slopes to the north; only one dip direction throughout.

🏜

Synclinal Valley

Gentle, subdued contours indicating non-resistant, already-eroded rock; two opposing dip directions.

⛰

Synclinal Ridge

Tightly spaced contours below 200 m asl, with two opposing dip directions across the ridge.

⛰

Anticlinal Ridge

Steep, high, resistant terrain with a clear scarp slope (dipping toward the valley) and dip slope (dipping away).

🏜

Anticlinal Valley

Relatively gentle relief over non-resistant, eroded rock, with two differing bedding dips.

🔥

Circular, widely-scattered hill contours; a structural lineament is visible around Kali Cacaban.

🏞

Fluvial Plain

Low-relief terrain with meandering channels and braided islands, showing mixed suspended- and bed-load sedimentation.

⛰

Anticlinal Hills

A mix of gentle and steep unevenly-scattered hills, again with two differing bedding dip directions.

Chapter III

Stratigraphy — Oldest to Youngest

Six rock units, each validated through direct outcrop description, HCl reaction tests, and fossil identification.

1. Melange Unit

Oldest

Sedimentary limestone & chert + metamorphic schist / phyllite

Formed by the subduction of low-density oceanic crust beneath higher-density continental crust. Rocks generated inside the subduction zone became chaotically mixed with sediments from outside it — white, fine-grained, HCl-reactive limestone bearing Nummulites fossils (deposited above the CCD), and hard, red, non-reactive chert (deposited below the CCD) — together with foliated (schist, phyllite) and non-foliated metamorphic rocks showing nematoblastic and granoblastic textures.

2. Claystone–Siltstone Unit

Claystone, siltstone, with limestone & conglomerate interbeds — Kali Pelikon

Deposited in a calm-water setting such as an open marine environment. Grey, loose claystone and black, loose siltstone interfinger, with occasional medium-grained, Nummulites-bearing limestone and conglomerate emplaced by gravity mass flow within the marine basin.

3. Breccia–Sandstone Unit

Fluvial breccia with sandstone interbeds — Kali Peniron

Formed in a river environment. Poorly-sorted, boulder-to-pebble breccia (grey, sand-to-clay matrix, sedimentary and igneous clasts) carries sandstone interbeds produced when turbulent, irregular river flow interrupted the heavier bedload deposition.

4. Sandstone–Claystone Unit

Interbedded turbidite sandstone & claystone — Kali Peniron

Fluctuating water energy produced alternating sandstone (medium-to-fine grain, light grey, parallel lamination and convolute bedding, moderate-to-good sorting) and dark-grey, loose claystone. The presence of a Bouma sequence points to deposition by turbidity currents.

5. Igneous Unit

Aphanitic igneous rock & diorite intrusion — Gunung Parang

A younger intrusion cutting through the claystone–siltstone unit, evidenced by sheet and columnar joints exposed at Gunung Parang. Black, fresh aphanitic rock sits alongside holocrystalline, phaneritic diorite bearing plagioclase, hornblende, quartz, and biotite.

6. Alluvial Deposit

Youngest

Unconsolidated fluvial deposits — Sungai Luk Ulo & Sungai Kedungbener

Deposited unconformably along the riverbanks from erosion and transport of every older unit — boulder-to-pebble fragments of igneous rock, sandstone, limestone, conglomerate, chert, green schist, phyllite, blue schist, marble, claystone, and siltstone, all lying flat and undeformed.

Chapter IV

Structural Geology

Two deformation styles are recorded in the field area: brittle deformation (faulting) and ductile deformation (folding).

Fault Structures

Brittle deformation

A fault plane was documented at Kali Pancur (strike/dip N168°E/90°SW) and again at Kali Soka (N119°E/73°SW). Slickenlines confirming fault-slip direction were found at Kali Soka (14°/N292°E, pitch 9°) and Kali Cacaban (2°/N330°E).

Fold Structure

Ductile deformation

An anticline is defined by contrasting bedding attitudes on its two limbs: the southern limb at Kali Jirek strikes/dips N50°E/80°SE, while the northern limb at Kali Pelikon strikes/dips N226°E/60°NW — together outlining the Karangsambung anticlinal valley.

Chapter V

Geological Synthesis

Putting the units and structures together into one formation history.

🌊

Subduction Mixing

The melange unit records chaotic mixing of subducted oceanic material with sediments from above and below the CCD, plus geodynamic metamorphism inside the zone.

🌊

Open-Marine Settling

Claystone-siltstone accumulated by suspended-load settling in calm marine water, punctuated by gravity-flow limestone and conglomerate pulses.

🏖

Fluvial Reworking

Strong river currents eroded and redeposited coarse breccia, with turbulent flow episodes leaving thin sandstone interbeds.

🌊

Turbidite Pulses

Fluctuating water energy and Bouma-sequence structures point to repeated turbidity-current deposition for the sandstone-claystone unit.

🔥

Intrusion

Igneous rock intruded the older claystone-siltstone unit last among the bedrock units, confirmed by sheet and columnar jointing at Gunung Parang.

💧

Modern Erosion

Ongoing river erosion and transport continues to rework fragments of every older unit into today's undeformed alluvial deposits.
